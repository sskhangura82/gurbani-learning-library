PL-1 · CURRENT APP BEHAVIOR

Your learning belongs to you

No account is needed to read the private library once the owner invitation is accepted. The reader does not request location, microphone, camera or contact access. Our reviewed source-only page contains no advertising SDK, analytics SDK, tracking pixel, or third-party script.

Your reading position, bookmarks, appearance preferences and reflections stay in your browser's local storage. You can export your learning from Reading tools. Clearing this site's data, or switching devices and browser profiles, may erase locally saved material unless you have exported it. Offline scripture packages also occupy browser caches.

Important limitation: the website is delivered through a temporary Cloudflare network tunnel and a Mac-hosted gateway. Although that gateway disables its ordinary request log, network and hosting providers can process connection metadata such as IP addresses, user agents and timestamps. We have not audited those providers' internal retention. We therefore do not claim that absolutely no information is processed or logged anywhere.

There is no server endpoint for uploading reflections in this reviewed application, and no in-app AI chatbot. Draft teaching was prepared during development, not generated from a reader's private questions. Your personal reflections are not uploaded to the source-code repository by the reader.

Choosing a third-party official source, such as the separate Hukamnama link below, leaves the library. That destination applies its own network and privacy practices. We neither embed its player nor silently fetch its page while you read Nitnem.

Privacy status: checked against this code and gated-preview configuration. Third-party infrastructure behavior is not certified; a permanent-host privacy review remains open.

PL-2 · TODAY'S ACCESS POLICY

Scripture and learning without a price tag

Reading, existing Meaning and Learn drafts, local bookmarks and source-review offline reading are free in this version. No subscription, in-app purchase, advertisement, score, badge or return-day streak is required.

Our product intention is to keep Gurbani reading and learning accessible without paywalls. A permanent, legally binding free forever public commitment and any future editorial licensing model are founder-reserved decisions, not already ratified by this private test build.

There are no hidden tiers of “more spiritual” practice. Learning is offered as an aid to understanding, not a reward for returning every day.

RD-9 / PL-6 · TESTED VS NOT YET TESTED

Made to be readable; testing continues

The private reader has large adjustable Gurmukhi (24–60 CSS pixels), named sections, visible previous/next controls, language markings, focus styles, and reduced-motion styling. Chrome phone-sized viewport and source-navigation checks passed in the engineering tests.

Not yet independently verified: physical iPhone Safari, VoiceOver in Punjabi, iOS Dynamic Type and full 200% browser text zoom, switch control, keyboard-only passage traversal on mobile, all dark/light color-contrast combinations, and real Home Screen/Airplane Mode operation. We do not call this a completed WCAG 2.2 AA audit or claim to be the first fully accessible Gurbani app.

Our intended standard is WCAG 2.2 AA, including 200% text resizing without loss, appropriate contrast, and usable controls. The 44-pixel minimum touch size is a design target rather than the actual WCAG 2.2 minimum requirement.

For corrections or accessibility feedback, contact the project's maintainer through an already established private channel. Do not post private reflection files, passwords or other personal information to a public issue or comment.

RD-11 · SOURCE CUSTODY

Correction questions stay visible

We maintain an issue record when digital versions disagree. The exact source text and its hash remain unchanged until an independently reviewed, versioned decision is made. A hash check proves the stored bytes have not changed—it cannot alone prove the wording is correct.

The current register includes the open Japji Sahib Pauri 13 comparison, Anand Sahib Pauri 24 Unicode hold, Jaap Sahib digital differences and variant questions in Chaupai and Rehras. The register distinguishes OPEN source questions from confirmed corrections; an open question does not establish a transcription error.
