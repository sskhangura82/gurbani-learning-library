# Meditation and breathing room: research and editorial decisions

Research checked: **28 November 2026**. Scope: an isolated Gurbani Learning Library candidate for review, not a clinical intervention or a production release.

## What this increment is for

Give someone an understandable visual breathing guide, with an immediate exit and a choice of comfortable rhythms. Give meditation and philosophical reflection their own space, with named sources and clear attribution. The interface must not equate a breathing animation with a complete traditional practice, turn a physiological response into a health promise, or synchronize Gurbani/Naam Simran to the pacer.

The candidate offers three paced patterns: even 5:5 breathing, longer-exhale 4:6 breathing, and optional box 4:4:4:4 breathing. A shorter-count setting is an explicitly labelled comfort adaptation. Natural-breath attention is an untimed introduction in the meditation room, not another paced protocol. Existing Simran remains a separate destination.

The research guide is `practice-sources.html`. The summaries below are modern editorial summaries, not quotations or authorized traditional instruction. “Research-informed” describes the selection process; it does not mean this app has been clinically validated.

## Evidence matrix

| Source | Protocol and population | Finding and material limits | Decision for this candidate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Marchant et al., 2025][marchant] | 84 college students; counterbalanced pacer comparison of 5:5, 4:6, 4:4:4:4 and 4:7:8; ten-minute conditions including training. | Generally larger acute HRV changes with six breaths per minute; no meaningful mood or blood-pressure benefit; mild overbreathing. One-session student sample. | Directly studied timing; no validation of this app or proof of lasting benefit. |
| [Fincham, Strauss & Cavanagh, 2023][coherent] | 400 adults randomized to approximately 5.5 or 12 breaths per minute, with equal inhale/exhale lengths, about ten minutes daily for four weeks. | Both groups improved, but the slower rate had no measurable additional mental-health or wellbeing benefit over the credible active comparison, including follow-up. | Do not claim a unique stress benefit from six breaths per minute or use “resonance” as though we measured a personal rate. |
| [Yilmaz Balban et al., 2023][balban] | 108 adults; five minutes daily for one month of box breathing, cyclic sighing, hyperventilation with retention, or mindfulness. Box timing was individualized. | Cyclic sighing showed the clearest positive-affect advantage over mindfulness. Box did not establish that advantage. Sleep, resting heart rate and HRV did not significantly improve. Exploratory, retrospectively registered trial with small groups and limited follow-up. | Box is optional and its evidence is described as limited. Do not infer that one sigh instantly resets the nervous system or that a 4:6 pacer reproduces cyclic sighing. |
| [Fincham et al., 2023 meta-analysis][review] | Stress analysis: twelve randomized trials, 785 adults, varied breathing interventions. | Modest average improvement in reported stress; most trials had bias concerns, with insufficient longer-term follow-up. | Broad evidence supports cautious exploration, not a guarantee for each named technique, timing, or person. |
| [McAllister et al., 2026][mcallister] | 66 adults, 22 per arm; supervised visual box 4:4:4:4, periodic prolonged exhalation or normal breathing; five minutes before and one minute during a modified virtual stress task. | Box and prolonged exhalation attenuated increases in heart rate, state anxiety and salivary alpha-amylase. No group effects on HRV, cortisol or cognition. Small, acute laboratory study. | Add promising direct box evidence without promising everyday or lasting benefits. The periodic exhale protocol was not our continuous 4:6 pattern. |

**Interpretation:** Immediate HRV changes, momentary mood reports, sustained wellbeing and treatment of a condition are different outcomes. The app makes no treatment, sleep-cure, trauma-release, oxygen-boosting or autonomic “reset” claim. The absence of a clear advantage in one study is not proof that nobody benefits; it is a reason to avoid overstating what is known.

The original methods and exact pacing were inspected in the [full article PDF][marchant-pdf]. Shortened counts, skip-hold behavior, user-chosen sessions and the interface are adaptations, not replicas. We do not reproduce the study's supervised setting or screening.

A [September 2026 box-breathing systematic review][box-review26] was also checked. Its accessible publisher abstract contains inconsistent trial counts (eight eligible studies, later twenty-nine), and the full text was not accessible in this pass. That unresolved reporting issue is recorded rather than silently corrected. We do not use it to assign a stronger evidence label; its cited McAllister trial was inspected separately at the primary source.

## User guidance and comfort decisions

[NHS guidance][nhs] supports gentle, comfortable breathing without forcing a target count. It also recognizes that a five-count breath may not initially suit someone. The candidate therefore makes comfort more important than matching a visual cue.

- Default to a rhythm with no holds. Choosing box must reveal its two holds before starting.
- Give “Stop” a persistent, reachable control. Stopping returns the person to ordinary breathing; completion is not a performance goal.
- Offer a shorter count and a way to leave holds. The resulting no-hold pattern must be named accurately instead of continuing to call it box breathing.
- Do not ask for maximal inhalation, complete emptying of the lungs, the slowest possible breath or endurance through air hunger.
- Keep the instruction concise: breathe gently; stop if dizzy, short of breath or more distressed; return to usual breathing.
- Keep the guide silent. Do not collect breathing counts, session history, medical information, completion scores or streaks.
- Use clear phase text as well as animation. Reduced motion must not remove the instructions.
- Stop or suspend guidance when the page becomes hidden. Resuming must not instruct an unexpected mid-cycle hold.
- Provide an untimed alternative. If attending to breathing is uncomfortable, someone can look around the room or leave the exercise.

These are conservative product decisions, not a clinical screening tool. [NCCIH][nccih] notes that meditation research includes unwanted experiences and that safety data are limited. We must not promise that meditation always relaxes people. Study exclusions are not silently converted into a universal medical contraindication list for all of our different practices.

## Techniques researched for the later catalogue

These entries are context for future review. They are not additional runnable exercises in this candidate and are not step-by-step instructions.

| Practice | Evidence reviewed | Why it needs its own implementation review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cyclic sighing | The Balban trial above tested repeated daily practice, not an isolated “instant reset.” | It uses a different breathing maneuver from a longer-exhale pacer. Instructions, tolerability and exact protocol must be reviewed separately. |
| 4–7–8 | [Vierra et al., 2022][478] studied immediate physiological measures in 43 young adults with and without sleep deprivation. That design did not establish a sleep-onset benefit. | It includes a longer retention. Do not market it as a sleep treatment or assume that familiar branding means stronger evidence. |
| Alternate-nostril breathing | A [2026 crossover trial][nostril] in 45 participants compared nostril-regulated practices, breath awareness and quiet rest. Anxiety decreased in several conditions, including rest; some physiological findings depended on baseline nostril dominance. | Requires accurate physical instruction and a named traditional context. Do not claim that it balances brain hemispheres. |
| Humming / Bhrāmarī | A [2025 pilot][humming25] involved sixteen adults. A [2026 randomized-session study][humming26] involved twenty-four; subjective ratings did not differ significantly, and breathing pattern could explain effects attributed to vibration. | Small, acute studies are not evidence of a lasting clinical effect. Avoid translating nitric-oxide or vibration findings into broad health promises. |
| Rapid or forceful breathing and extended retention | These methods differ materially from comfortable slow pacing. | They are outside this gentle, self-guided increment. Adding more names is not sufficient reason to add an executable protocol. |

For each future method, record its exact instruction, source, population, comparator, duration, measured outcomes, limitations, comfort considerations and adaptation status. A technique can be historically important while its modern health evidence remains uncertain.

## Traditional sources and attribution

“Eastern philosophy” covers many distinct traditions. We will name the tradition and source instead of merging their vocabulary or goals. A historical text supports a claim about that text; it does not by itself validate a medical benefit. Likewise, a physiological study does not validate a metaphysical claim.

### Buddhist mindfulness of breathing

[Ānāpānasati Sutta, MN 118][mn118], in **Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu's translation**, includes awareness of long and short breaths within a much wider training in body, feelings, mind and contemplative themes. The candidate's natural-breath introduction is modern and deliberately modest; it is not the complete sixteen-step practice or a claim to represent every Buddhist school.

### Yoga and prāṇāyāma

[Yoga Sūtra 2.49][yoga49] and the following sūtras place breath regulation in a wider path of practice. The linked edition is **Rama Prasada's 1924 translation with commentaries**; translators and commentators must remain distinguishable. Our three modern paced patterns are not presented as exact prescriptions from that passage, and box breathing is not relabelled as an ancient technique without source support.

### Stoic reflection

[Epictetus, Handbook / Enchiridion, chapter 1][epictetus], in **Elizabeth Carter's translation**, distinguishes what depends on us from what does not. An optional reflection about one's next action can draw on that distinction. It is a philosophical reflection, not an ancient breathing prescription or a statement that illness or hardship is a person's fault.

### Sikh remembrance

Keep the existing [Simran room](../simran.html), Gurbani learning and Sikh sources separately identified. The official [Sikh Rehat Maryada][sgpc] is the source destination for further study; Articles III–IV were identified through its index/search representation, while full-PDF retrieval was limited during this research pass. We therefore do not introduce a new detailed doctrinal summary or instruction from inaccessible text. This candidate adds no breathing-to-Naam synchronization and makes no claim that all Sikh practice excludes attention to breath.

Traditional interpretation and any fuller teaching course still require specialist editorial review. That does not prevent a carefully attributed source guide and an explicitly modern introductory exercise from being reviewed now.

## Commercial comparison and the product direction

Official product pages checked on 28 November 2026:

| Product | Verified feature information | Implication for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Calm][calm] | Its help page lists six breathing styles, 4/6/8-breaths-per-minute settings, durations from one minute to open-ended, separate guidance/background audio controls and device-specific haptics settings. | An animated pacer and adjustable timing are established features, not a new differentiator. |
| [Headspace][headspace] | Its official listing offers a five-minute box-breathing activity. | Naming box breathing alone does not make our room distinctive. |

These pages were not a comprehensive hands-on audit. They do not establish that either service lacks research, accessibility or traditional context. We make no claim that this candidate is already larger or more effective.

Our proposed distinction is an inspectable connection between the exercise and its evidence: exact source links, plain limitations, explicit adaptation labels, named traditional texts and translators, and a choice to practice without scoring or stored history. A larger catalogue should follow this standard, not weaken it.

## Review and delivery boundaries

This document records research and design decisions. Runtime behavior, accessibility, browser compatibility, offline behavior and source preservation need engineering verification against the final candidate. No study cited here tested this application. A successful browser test verifies the guide's behavior, not a health outcome.

The current increment should be reviewed as a bounded room foundation: three optional paced patterns, an untimed natural-breath introduction, respectful source-linked contexts and the evidence guide. Human narration, additional practices, fuller traditional courses and clinical claims are separate work.
