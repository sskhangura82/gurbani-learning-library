## What the register records for each recording

Reciter or institution · the work · the catalogue's own title · category · catalogue pages (never a media file) · how I know · tradition where stated · **what is unknown** · whom to approach · permission (status and the scopes granted) · printed edition recited from · whether coverage has been verified · checksum of the cleared file · timings · review by a Santhiya teacher.

The last six are empty for every entry, because none of that work can begin before permission.

## Three kinds of recitation, and three things that are not recitation

- **Santhiya** — slow and exact, for learning pronunciation.
- **Continuous Paath** — the traditional pace of a Paath.
- **Alternative recitation** — another authentic voice for the same Bani.
- **Daily Mukhwak, Katha, Kirtan** — named separately and never offered in place of the three above.

Where only one cleared recording exists, the other places stay empty. Nothing is substituted.

## What permission has to say before a recording is used

For each recording, in writing, from a named person who is entitled to give it:

1. May the library **link** to the provider's page? (Lowest ask; often already welcome.)
2. May it **stream** or **embed** from the provider?
3. May it **host a copy**? May a listener keep an **offline copy**?
4. May it play **one line at a time**, or **repeat** a line?
5. May it **make line timings** for this recording, and **publish** them?
6. Where, for how long, and how can permission be withdrawn?
7. How should the reciter and custodian be credited?
8. What conditions of respect does the custodian set? (No advertising beside it; no alteration; no use to train or imitate a voice — the library commits to these unasked.)

A grant of (1) is not a grant of (3) or (5). The register stores each scope separately.
