## Draft 1 — to the custodian of Bhagat Jaswant Singh Ji's recordings

*To be addressed once the right person is known. Start by asking Gursevak and Damdami Taksal who that is.*

> Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki Fateh.
>
> I am [name], writing from [place]. With a small group I am building the Gurbani Learning Library, a free reading and learning aid for Nitnem and Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji. It has no advertising, no subscription, no accounts and no tracking, and it will stay that way.
>
> We would like to help learners follow a careful recitation of Japji Sahib line by line: the line being recited is shown, and a learner can pause, go back and hear one line again. For this we need one recitation made for learning. Bhagat Jaswant Singh Ji's recitation is the one we would most wish to ask about.
>
> Before doing anything at all with it, we ask:
>
> 1. Who holds the right to give permission for this recording? If it is not you, could you tell us whom to ask?
> 2. Would the custodian be willing for the library to use **one recording, Japji Sahib only**, as a first trial? We would ask separately before any further use.
> 3. If so, which of these would be acceptable: a link to your own page only; playing it from your site; keeping a copy so that it works without a connection; playing one line at a time; our making and publishing a list of the times at which each line begins?
> 4. Which printed edition was the recitation made from?
> 5. How should Bhagat Ji and the custodian be credited, and what conditions of respect should we keep?
>
> We undertake, whatever the answer: not to alter the recording; not to place anything commercial beside it; never to use it to imitate or generate a voice; to remove it at once if asked; and to show you the result before anyone else sees it.
>
> If the answer is no, we will not use it, and we thank you for considering it.
>
> [name, contact]

## Draft 2 — to the Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee, Sri Amritsar

> Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki Fateh.
>
> I am [name], writing from [place], about the daily Mukhwak from Sachkhand Sri Harmandir Sahib published on the Committee's website.
>
> We are building the Gurbani Learning Library, a free learning aid with no advertising, accounts or tracking. We would like learners to be able to read the day's Mukhwak in Gurmukhi, with the date as the Committee gives it, and to be sent to the Committee's own page to hear the recitation and the Katha.
>
> We ask the Committee's guidance on four points:
>
> 1. May the library show the Gurmukhi of the day's Mukhwak, naming the Committee as the source and linking to its page?
> 2. Is there a preferred way to learn each morning which Shabad was read, so that we never show a wrong one? If no reading is available to us we will show none.
> 3. May we link to the Committee's page for the recitation and the Katha? We would not copy, host or alter the audio.
> 4. Are there terms we should follow for the Punjabi explanation and English translation shown on the page? At present we take neither.
>
> We would be grateful also to know whom to write to in future.
>
> [name, contact]
