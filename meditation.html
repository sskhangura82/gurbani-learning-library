Traditions & sources

Explore their context.

Each tradition has its own purpose and language. These short introductions name the source and explain how it relates to this room.

Sikh remembranceNaam, scripture & daily life

The Sikh Rehat Maryada places remembrance of Naam and scripture alongside living according to the Gurus’ teaching and voluntary service. The Simran Room preserves that Sikh frame; its fuller teaching remains under development.

Source: SGPC, Sikh Rehat Maryada, Articles III–IV.

Visit the existing Simran Room
Mindfulness of breathingAn early Buddhist text · MN 118

MN 118 begins with awareness of breathing and develops into a wider path involving body, feelings, mind and insight. Our natural-breath prompts are a modern introduction inspired by that starting point. The full text has a much wider religious purpose.

Source: Majjhima Nikāya 118, translated by Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu.

Breath in Classical YogaPatañjali · Yoga Sūtras 2.49–53

These passages discuss regulating and suspending breath within a wider discipline, connected with preparing the mind for concentration. They give historical context. Our exact on-screen timings are modern practice settings, with their own evidence and limits.

Source: Yoga Sūtras 2.49–53, Rama Prasada translation, 1924.

Choice and responseA Stoic reflection · Epictetus

In Handbook 1, Epictetus distinguishes our judgments and actions from outcomes beyond our command. This is a philosophical invitation to reflect; it makes no claim about the effects of a breathing exercise.

An optional modern reflection: What is one action available to me now?

Source: Epictetus, Handbook 1, Elizabeth Carter translation.
