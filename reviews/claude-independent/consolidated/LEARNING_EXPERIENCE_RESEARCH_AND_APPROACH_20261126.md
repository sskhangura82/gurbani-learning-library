# Scholarly, enjoyable and easy: research and a proposed approach

**From:** Claude (AI system; independent reviewer on GLP-001). **For:** the owner, to pass to ChatGPT. **Date:** 26 November 2026.
**Asked:** how to make this "a highly scholarly yet fun, interactive, easy-to-use" learning application, possibly with artificial intelligence in it later.
**Method:** web research on 26 November 2026 (sources at the end), read against what is in this repository. I looked at search results and public pages; I did not use the other products hands-on.

---

## 1. What the best comparable libraries actually do

| Who | What works there | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Sefaria** (free, open-source Jewish library) | Every text is linked to the others. Tapping a passage opens a side panel of connected texts, topics, study sheets and audio. People assemble passages into their own "source sheets" for teaching. | This is the model for "everything connected". The side panel and the teaching sheet are the two ideas most worth borrowing. |
| **Quran.com** | Tap a single word to hear it and see its meaning — by its own account one of its most used features. Text follows the recitation. Short daily "learning plans" that need no registration. A "report a problem" link on every verse's translation. | Word-tap, follow-along and short guided paths are what make study feel alive. The report link turns every reader into a proof-reader. |
| **The Guru Granth Sahib Project** (Sikh Research Institute) | Word-by-word research on etymology, grammar and meaning, then a literal translation and an interpretive one, released Bani by Bani. The institute describes it as a twenty-year effort. | Real scholarship at the level of the word takes decades and a team. We should seek to work with it and link to it, not imitate it with AI drafts. |
| **Learning research** | Six study methods have strong, repeated support: spacing practice out, mixing topics, recalling from memory, explaining in one's own words, concrete examples, and pairing words with pictures. | "Fun and interactive" has a scientific core: let the learner try to recall, come back later, and see as well as read. |

What none of them shows us: a way to make AI-written religious teaching trustworthy without human scholars. Nobody has one.

## 2. What the Panth's institutions have said about AI

This must shape any use of AI here.

- SGPC has written to the large AI companies asking them to stop spreading distorted versions of Gurbani and Sikh history, after complaints from the sangat.
- SGPC has published a directive, issued in pursuance of an order of the Panj Singh Sahiban at Sri Akal Takht Sahib of 28 December 2025, that visual, animated, AI-generated or digitally altered representations of the Gurus or of Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji are impermissible.
- SGPC has set up a task force and a policy on AI-made content.

So three lines are not ours to cross: **no AI-written Gurbani, no AI voice reciting it, no AI pictures of the Gurus.** The project's own rules already say this. A Sikh learning library that uses AI carelessly will be rejected by the people it is for.

## 3. What happened when other faiths put AI in front of readers

- **Sefaria** released an AI "library assistant" in August 2026, built on Claude. It is tied to the library's own texts, watched by people and by automated checks, tested against a written exam before release, and designed to send the learner into the sources. Sefaria still says plainly that it can be wrong.
- **Magisterium AI** (Catholic) was tested by a Catholic magazine against a theologian. It was well-read and sometimes simply wrong; a later critique found it presenting opinion as settled teaching.
- A recurring problem everywhere: models **misquote scripture word for word**. The safe design is that the AI never types scripture. It fetches the stored line.

Lesson: grounding and guardrails help. They do not remove the need for reviewed content underneath, and the name on the door ("official", "authentic") must not promise more than the machine can keep.

## 4. The approach I propose

### One sentence

**Every screen is one passage of Gurbani, and everything else is one tap deeper** — a word, a sound, a meaning, a question, a connection, a source.

### The learning loop

**Read → Listen → Understand → Try → Reflect → Connect.**
The app has Read, part of Understand, and Reflect. "Try" (recalling for yourself) and "Connect" are what turn a reader into a learner, and they are missing.

### What "fun and interactive" should mean here

Enjoyment comes from discovery and from feeling oneself learn. It does not come from points.

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| **Tap any word** for its meaning, and later its sound | The single most used study feature elsewhere |
| **Follow the recitation** line by line; repeat one line | Already the owner's favourite idea (the moving light) |
| **Three depths** on one switch: Simple · Detailed · Deep | Already the project's standard |
| **"Check yourself"**: cover the meaning and recall it; match a word to its meaning; find the Rahao; put the stanzas in order | Recalling is the best-supported way to learn. Questions are made by fixed rules from the reviewed lesson, not by AI. Private. Nothing is scored against anyone. |
| **Come back later**: "five words from last week" | Spacing. Offered, never nagged. |
| **Pictures with words**: the shape of a Bani, where the Rahao sits, a map when history arrives | Pairing words with pictures helps everyone |
| **Connections panel** on any line: related Gurbani, the word, the idea, the history, the sound | The Sefaria idea |
| **Study sheets**: a parent or teacher gathers passages and notes into one printable page | For gurdwara classes and families. No account; saved on the device and exported. |
| **Short paths**: "Understand your Japji Sahib, ten minutes a day" | Structure for people who do not know where to start |
| **"Report a problem"** on every line | Readers become proof-readers |

**Do not:** streaks, points, badges, rankings, social feeds, reminders that guilt. The owner has ruled these out, and they would cheapen the thing. Private marks of completion ("you have read all 38 Pauris") are fine.

### What "scholarly" should mean here

- Every statement shows where it comes from and who has reviewed it. "AI-assisted draft, not yet reviewed" is said on the screen until a qualified person changes it.
- Scripture, literal meaning, teaching, comparison, science and reflection look different and are never blended.
- Where learned people disagree, the disagreement is shown.
- Work with those who have done the scholarship — the Sikh Research Institute, Khalis Foundation, Shabad OS, SGPC — and link to them.

### What "easy" should mean here

Four entrances and no more: **Today · Read · Explore · Saved.** One thumb. Large Gurmukhi. Nothing appears on the home screen until it has reviewed content behind it. An elder and a child should each manage without being shown.

## 5. Where AI fits: three rings around a centre

The owner's own principle from his other work applies exactly: **intelligence cannot manufacture authority.** AI may find, sort and explain. Authority stays with Gurbani and with qualified people.

| | What AI may do | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Centre — Gurbani and its recitation** | **Nothing.** It never writes, changes, voices or pictures it. | Fixed. Not open to revision. |
| **Ring 1 — the workshop** | Draft lessons, check each other's work, find errors, prepare material for human reviewers. Nothing reaches a reader unreviewed and unlabelled. | In use today |
| **Ring 2 — the librarian** | Understand what a person is looking for and bring them the right *stored, reviewed* passages and lessons. It writes no religious sentences of its own. Small models for this can now run inside the phone's browser, so the question need never leave the device. | Recommended next, after the repairs |
| **Ring 3 — the study companion** | Answer a learner's question **only** by quoting and linking reviewed material in the library. Gurbani is fetched from the stored text, never typed. If the library has nothing reviewed, it says so. It is labelled as a helper, not a teacher and never the Guru's voice. It points a person in distress to people. Opt-in, and outside the scripture reader. | Later, and only when there is enough reviewed material to quote. Needs the owner's decision: it either sends questions to a server (cost, privacy) or runs a weaker model on the phone. |

Two cautions on Ring 3. A companion with nothing reviewed to quote can only make things up, so it must come after the content. And it must pass a written examination — set by qualified people, including trick questions — before any reader meets it, as Sefaria did.

On the phone itself: in 2026 browsers on current phones can run small AI models without a server. A small chat model is a download of several hundred megabytes and is noticeably weaker than the large ones, more so in Punjabi. That is enough for Ring 2 (finding). I would not trust it with Ring 3 (explaining) yet.

## 6. The order of work

1. **Finish the repairs and see them on a real phone.** Trust first. (Phase 0 in the roadmap beside this file.)
2. **Make one passage wonderful, end to end** — "the golden lesson" below — and put it in front of five real people. Learn what delights and what confuses before building more.
3. **Find the people.** One qualified Gurbani reader, one native Punjabi reader, one Santhiya teacher. Write to the Sikh Research Institute and SGPC as would-be partners.
4. **Then grow at the reviewers' pace**, with Ring 2 as the first AI a reader touches.

## 7. For ChatGPT — the golden lesson

**Take one Shabad the project already holds exactly: Gujri Mahalla 1, Ang 504–505** (reader in #43; draft lesson with 56 headwords in #44). Build nothing new underneath. Make this one screen the best the library can be.

| # | The learner can… | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | tap any of the 56 headwords and see its meaning without losing their place | 56 of 56 open and close; reading position unchanged |
| 2 | switch Simple · Detailed · Deep, in English or Punjabi | each level shows; the Gurmukhi never moves or changes |
| 3 | see the Rahao marked, with one line on why it is the centre | present in every view, including print |
| 4 | "check yourself": five questions made by rule from the lesson (match word and meaning; find the Rahao; order the stanzas) | every question has exactly one right answer taken from the lesson; nothing is stored except on the device; no score is shown as a rank |
| 5 | open a connections panel: the nine related lines of Gurbani, two ideas (Naam, Kirpa), comparisons folded away | every link lands on the right line |
| 6 | see, on every block, its source and "AI-assisted draft, not yet reviewed" | no block without one |
| 7 | write a private reflection and export it | survives reload and comes back from the export unchanged |
| 8 | be sent to SGPC's page to listen | link only; no audio in the app |
| 9 | report a problem with any line | produces a message naming the exact line |
| 10 | use it at 320 and 390 wide, with large text, by keyboard, with a screen reader's labels in place | automated checks clean; **then five real people**: a newcomer, a child, a parent, an elder, a regular reader. Each is asked afterwards, "What did you learn?" Their words are recorded as said. |

It stays a private draft. I review it against criteria written before I open it.

## 8. The name

Naming is the owner's. Three thoughts:
- **Keep "Gurbani Learning Library".** It is plain, true, and will still be true when history and language are added. This is my recommendation.
- If a wider name is wanted later, look for one from the tradition's own vocabulary of learning, chosen with a Punjabi speaker and checked for existing use.
- **Do not put "AI" in the name.** It would date quickly and would worry the very people the library hopes to serve.

## 9. What I did not do

I did not use Sefaria, Quran.com or the Guru Granth Sahib Project hands-on; this rests on their public pages and on search results. I did not test any AI model on Gurbani. One figure often repeated — that AI models misquote scripture in a sizeable share of cases — I found only second-hand and have not relied on it.
