Subject: Permission request — featuring your Gurbani articles in a free family learning app

Dear T. Singh,

I have been reading gurbani.org for some time. Your articles explain Gurbani
in a way I have not found elsewhere in English, and I am writing to ask your
permission for something specific.

I am building a Gurbani learning application for my own family and a few close
friends. It is free, has no advertising, no subscriptions, no accounts, no
tracking and no analytics, and it will stay that way. It is not a business and
will never be sold. It carries the Gurmukhi text of Japji Sahib, Sukhmani
Sahib and the Nitnem banis, checked line by line against a published digital
reference, with every discrepancy recorded rather than quietly corrected.

What I would like to ask:

1. May I include a selection of your articles in the application, reproduced
   in full and unaltered, under your name, with a link to the original article
   on gurbani.org on every one?
2. May a reader print an article from the application for their own personal
   use?
3. May the articles be stored on the reader's own device so they can be read
   without an internet connection? Nothing would be uploaded, shared or
   collected.

What I would not do without asking you again separately:

- Edit, abridge, paraphrase or rewrite your articles.
- Generate new articles in your style, by any means, including with AI tools.
  Your words would be your words, or they would not be in the application.
- Present your articles as the application's own teaching, or as an
  authoritative interpretation of Gurbani. You write on your About page that
  you are a learner and claim no scholarly standing, and I would carry that
  framing through: your articles would be labelled as your reflections, under
  your name, kept clearly distinct from the scripture text itself.
- Use your material commercially in any form.

If you would prefer a narrower permission — particular articles only, online
only, or no offline storage — I would be glad to work within whatever you set.
If you would rather not, I will simply link to gurbani.org instead and
reproduce nothing.

Thank you for thirty years of this work.

With respect,
Sarwinder (Steve) Khangura
