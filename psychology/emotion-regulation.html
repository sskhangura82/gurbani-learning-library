Two approaches researchers studied

Reappraisal involves reconsidering how a situation is understood. Distraction shifts attention to something else. Sheppes and colleagues studied people’s choices between these approaches when responding to negative emotional stimuli. [1: Introduction, PDF pp. 1–2]

Across three small experiments, participants more often chose reappraisal with lower-intensity stimuli and distraction with higher-intensity stimuli. [1: Experiments 1–3, PDF pp. 3–5]

Where the evidence stops

The study examined choices between two supplied strategies in controlled tasks. It does not establish the best response for every everyday situation, long-term outcomes, or clinical treatment effects. The authors identify goals, available cognitive resources and longer-term adaptation as questions for further study. [1: General Discussion, PDF p. 5]

Optional reflection

Choose a mild, ordinary frustration you are comfortable considering, such as finding a passage difficult. You can skip the exercise.

  1. What happened? What meaning did you add to it?
  2. Is another interpretation consistent with the facts? What remains unknown?
  3. What is your immediate goal, and what possible next step would fit it?

An example for study

Suppose a word in a passage is unfamiliar. Describe the specific gap in your understanding, then decide whether to look up the word, ask a question or return to it later. This example is an original reflection aid; the paper did not test it.
