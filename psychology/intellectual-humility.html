What the research examines

Intellectual humility concerns recognising limits in what you know and the possibility of being mistaken. Porter and colleagues’ review found varied definitions and measures, with awareness of intellectual limitations a recurring feature. Their framework also distinguishes private awareness from its expression. [1: pp. 577 and 581, Table 3 and Figure 3]

In a separate paper, four studies linked self-reported intellectual humility with learning behaviours such as persistence and choosing challenging material. A fifth study randomly assigned adults to read about intellectual humility or intellectual certainty; their tutorial choices after feedback differed between the groups. [2: Studies 1–5, pp. 2–7]

Where the evidence stops

The first four studies were correlational. Humility was measured through self-report, and the experiment concerned a short task. The authors note uncertainty about whether their manipulation changed private awareness or self-presentation. These results do not establish lasting learning gains from this page or its questions. [2: Study 5 and §9.1, pp. 7 and 9]
