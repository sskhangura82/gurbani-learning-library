Attention while reading

ਪੜ੍ਹਦੇ ਸਮੇਂ ਧਿਆਨ

At a natural stopping point, ask what you understood and where the thread became unclear.

Intellectual humilityAttention while readingEmotion regulation

What the researchers did

Unsworth and McMillan asked 150 university participants to read an academic text, report what their attention was on at several points, and answer comprehension questions. They also measured working-memory capacity, interest, motivation and prior topic experience. [1: Method, pp. 835–836]

More reports of thoughts unrelated to the reading task were associated with lower comprehension. The relationship remained in the researchers’ statistical model alongside other measured factors. [1: Results, p. 838; Figure 2, p. 840]

Where the evidence stops

This was an individual-differences study using a particular academic reading task. Its associations do not establish causation, and it did not test the reflection questions below. It also does not establish that every kind of mind-wandering, in every setting, is harmful. [1: Method and Discussion, pp. 835–836 and 838–840]

Optional reflection

Try these questions after a short section of your own reading. You can answer silently or make a note elsewhere.

  1. What is one idea you can now explain without looking at the passage?
  2. Where did a word, sentence or connection become unclear?
  3. What would you like to clarify next: a word’s meaning, the link between two ideas, or the passage’s main question?

A small study choice

A reader reaches the end of a paragraph and cannot explain how its final sentence connects to the first. They mark that connection as a question and reread the relevant section. This is an original example for reflection, not a tested training protocol.

Alongside Gurbani learning

Use these questions to reflect on reading and understanding. The study did not investigate Gurbani or measure devotion. Meanings and theological questions belong with the source text and its own commentary.
